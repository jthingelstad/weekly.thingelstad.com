<h3><a href="https://www.logicmonitor.com/blog/avoid-front-page-news-outage-like-marketo/">How to avoid Marketo's outage and the twitter storm —LogicMonitor</a></h3>
<p>💬 Monitoring your DNS expiration and renewal with your registrar is very similar to monitoring SSL certificate renewal. I always monitor SSL certificates but have never monitored the domain name and registrar for registration. The failure cases seem small, and most business domains are on auto-renewal, but you could have a payment failure and an unmonitored email address case a big problem.</p>

<h3><a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/3048975/the-scientifically-proven-way-to-have-the-best-vacation-ever">The Scientifically Proven Way To Have The Best Vacation Ever</a></h3>
<p>💬 The last three items on this list are better in psychological data. How something <em>ends</em> is very impactful to how we remember it. 'Ending on a high note' will bias you to remembering any event better than it was. It's also fun to think of the 'memory value' of a vacation. In an odd way, vacations are an investment into your future as you remember them in the past.</p>

<p>💬 Nice add to the AWS toolset and clearly a win as customers, particularly large ones, use AWS accounts as an ultimate isolation and cost management tool.</p>
<h3><a href="http://thesweetsetup.com/apps/the-best-note-taking-apps-for-ios/">The Best Notes App for iPhone and iPad: Bear — The Sweet Setup</a></h3>
<p>💬 Nice overview of the notes app that I use for meeting notes. I've gotten comfortable with multiple notes locations, mainly Bear and Apple Notes. I use Bear for meetings and professional things, and Apple Notes for everything else. The tagging and automation features of Bear are a huge win in a work context, but the Note sharing and ease-of-use of Apple Notes wins out at home and for personal items. I actually really like segmenting these.</p>
<h3><a href="http://www.macdrifter.com/2017/07/managing-check-lists.html">Managing Check Lists —Macdrifter</a></h3>
<p>💬 Overview of a lot of different ways to manage lists, specifically focused on what the author is looking for. Useful overview of a bunch of different list managers. It seems like <a href="https://www.omnigroup.com/omnifocus">OmniFocus</a> with a project template solution would meet the requirements but that isn't listed in his solutions.</p>
<h3><a href="https://magmadaemon.org/">Magma</a></h3>
<p>💬 A completely secure, in transit and rest, open-source email system born from the ashes of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavabit">Lavabit</a>, which you may remember as the email service that <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden">Edward Snowden</a> used and which was shut down from legal threats and protecting it’s users email. I have long argued that email is no longer something 'citizens' can do, it's a professional activity filled with spam and virus threats. However, I'm also a big fan of encrypted email and massively distributed email. The more encryption and the more distributed email is (not centralized at one or two providers) the harder it is for people to harvest and monitor it. An open-source encrypted email system is a nice step in that direction. Even better would be for it to be containerized so you could run it with little technical knowledge.</p>
<h3><a href="https://ourworldindata.org/trust">Trust - Our World In Data</a></h3>
<p>💬 Huge article that I mostly skimmed for the graphs. I like the visuals, and the data itself is very interesting. Seems notable that trust in other people has droped 15% in last 40 years in the United States. It's also more variable than I would have thought. Compare that to Sweden. The entire site is full of stuff like this, you could spend a lot of time here. ✨ Thanks <a href="https://twitter.com/swensn">@swensn</a> for the link.</p>
<h3><a href="http://anniemueller.com/2017/07/22/the-rules-of-creation/">The Rules of Creation – a n n i e m u e l l e r</a></h3>
<p>💬 Great thoughts on how to think about the creative process. I think you could apply this to any creative endeavor, but I like to think about how it applies to the creation of software (which is a creative endeavor!).</p>
<h3><a href="https://www.amazon.com/p/feature/xde6cauvpfp66o2">Sustainability - Give Your Amazon Box New Life</a></h3>
<p>💬 I had no idea that your old Amazon boxes can be filled with things you no longer need and mailed to a charitable organization!</p>
<h3><a href="https://stripe.com/us/jobs/candidate-info-culture">A quick guide to Stripe’s culture</a></h3>
<p>💬 Technology firms have taken great effort to be very specific in thinking about their culture. I do this as well and think it is critical to organizations. As a result, the culture statements are often more verbose than you may think. This one from Stripe is interesting both for it’s verbosity as well as the 'Questions you should ask yourself' sections. Interesting content, I’m not sure about all of it but I like that they have a defined way to share feedback and it’s open to the public.</p>
<h3><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2016/02/28/magazine/what-google-learned-from-its-quest-to-build-the-perfect-team.html">What Google Learned From Its Quest to Build the Perfect Team</a></h3>
<p>💬 Long article that could use a TL;DR section but nothing hugely surprising here. Highlights the need for <strong>dependability</strong>, <strong>structure and clarity</strong>, <strong>meaning</strong>, <strong>impact</strong> and <strong>psychological safety</strong>.</p>
<h3><a href="http://pokemongolive.com/en/post/pokemongofestupdate">Niantic Pokémon GO Fest Chicago Update - Pokémon GO</a></h3>
<p>💬 The <a href="http://www.businessinsider.com/pokmon-go-chicago-event-issues-ticket-refunds-after-widespread-outage-2017-7">first ever Pokemon GO Fest</a> was in Chicago this last weekend. They took action on it right away but another data point on how hard it is to scale technology infrastructure and systems for events.</p>

<h4>by Shiny Frog</h4>
<p><em>Bear is a beautiful, flexible writing app for crafting notes, prose, code and now sketches!</em></p>
<p>I use Bear for one specific task, taking notes during meetings. I also use other applications for managing text but Bear's automation, tagging and sketching capabilities have made it a perfect tool for me to capture information and tasks during meetings. For example, I tag all notes with the week and day of the meeting, as well as the people in it. Want to see all meetings with a certain person? Easy with a tag. All notes from a given week? Another tag.</p>
