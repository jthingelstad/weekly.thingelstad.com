## Notable

### [How employment is changing at Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)

Some interesting data on how work at Anthropic is being changed as Claude takes on more and more of the actual effort. "By August interactive AI agent hours outnumbered employees six to one." I read that as about 85% of their "workforce" is Claude.

> This acceleration makes human attention the bottleneck for agent output. Even among non-technical employees, reading all the output of their Claude agents would take half their work week. In response, employees turn to "meta-work," of building visibility tools and monitoring agent pipelines.

The "Reading Claude as a fulltime job" graph is mind-blowing and a direct representation of the above bottleneck. Engineers are starting to see this as you release agentic processes you need the entire flow to be agentic or it slows down massively.

### [How tech workers are feeling in 2026: a workforce splitting in two](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-tech-workers-are-feeling-in-2026)

Technology has a pretty high burnout rate. I don't think it is discussed enough. With the massive transformation happening with AI we are seeing this push people in all sorts of ways.

> Tech workers are either amplified by AI or shaken by it, and that divide shapes their feelings about work more than any title, tenure, or company.

I wrote [Building and Puzzling about Software](https://www.thingelstad.com/2026/09/17/building-and-puzzling-about-software.html) and would suggest those "amplified" are the Builders and the "shaken by it" are the Puzzlers.

I believe that a lot of burnout in tech comes from the fact that you must always be learning, and learning is hard. After 30+ years in this field I know that is the one fact that has held true. You must always be learning the next thing. And at least for me as I get older that learning isn't as easy.

> Significant burnout rose from 44.7% to 55.7% of respondents, while career optimism fell from 54.8% to 48.7%. 

To be optimistic for a bit, I do think that we will get to the other side of this and return to a somewhat normalized rate of change. Each major transformation in tech has a high change curve where everything gets redefined, and then you stabilize and scale on the other side. The time of that change is directly linked to the scale of the change.

This one may take a while.

I am receptive to the concept that programming can be like art. An algorithm can be beautiful. But ultimately a program has to run and follow the laws of the runtime. Art does not. Productivity is a meaningful consideration with programming that really doesn't extend to art. Yes you can artfully program, but there is also a cost and performance consideration that has no analog in the world of art.

## The nnxyz rooms

Years ago I discovered the work of nnxyz on nifty.ink. I acquired a few of his pieces and grew to like it more and more. I ended up collecting four complete collections of hundreds of pieces. I had always wished to create a gallery like experience for anyone to experience. With the help of AI I was able to make that vision a reality.

**Check out [the nnxyz rooms](https://nnxyz.thingelstad.xyz).**

Some highlights of what the website has.

- **Every drawing can be watched being made.** The 574 Nifty Ink works are rebuilt from their archived brush strokes as native vector images, and each one replays stroke by stroke, with the recorded order, widths, colors and transparency.
- **Hung like a museum, not a feed.** Six wings, 28 rooms, 626 works. Every work hangs in exactly one room, rooms hold 20 to 30 works, and each room is hung in the order the works were drawn, so a walk through a room is a walk through a few weeks of drawing.
- **Wall text in a museum voice.** Each room has an introduction, chapters, labels, one-line notes, explained comparisons and a closing.
- **An audio tour in every room.** Press play and a voice reads the room while each work comes on screen, about seven minutes a room, with pause, skip and a progress line.
- **A screen saver that draws.** One work at a time, drawn stroke by stroke on black, held with its caption, then the next. Fullscreen, with your choice of drawing speed and hold time, and it can be limited to one room.
- **A map of the building.** The collection as a three-floor museum map, rooms sized by how many works they hold, colored by collection, with doors between consecutive rooms and stairs between floors.
- **Readable by machines too.** `/llms.txt` indexes the site for language models and `/llms-full.txt` carries the complete gallery text in one file.
- **Light or dark.** A white gallery by default, black by choice, remembered across visits. The artwork itself is never altered.
- **Everything downloadable.** Each artwork page offers the native SVG, the original PNG and the archived metadata and provenance records, with a link to the collected NFT.

I hope you like browsing and experiencing nnxyz's work as much as I do. I feel like it has real movement and energy in it. nnxyz himself uses the term "thrash-art" — I dig it! I've never liked the VR gallery experiences online and I think this museum walk through, particularly with audio guidance, is a better way to experience it.
