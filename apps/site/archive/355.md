### [AWS Emulator and the engineer's job when agents write the code](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2026/11/aws-emulator-and-the-engineers-job-when-agents-write-the-code.html)

This is super interesting for a few different reasons. Let me give you my read. 

1. It isn't disclosed but a huge amount of AWS usage must be agents at this time.
2. Agents need cheap ways to make mistakes.
3. AWS [built this using swarms of agents](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/how-autonomous-ai-agents-built-aws-emulator) committing very fast.

If you are reading this and you have never logged into something like the AWS console imagine an incredibly powerful tool surface with hundreds of products and incredible complexity. I'm pretty technical, and there is no way I can make my way around in the depths of AWS. However, Claude has changed all that. I have now moved nearly every project I run to AWS. Every website I have runs through it. There is no way I could have done this before but Claude knows AWS super well and can manage it. I'm not unique. I'm sure that a huge amount of AWS usage is now a coding agent on the other side. It will be great for them to use an emulator for the entire cloud to test.

When agents are testing today they have to do it in AWS. With this emulator AWS is both reducing the time and risk of a mistake, but also making it cheaper for them. Nothing real is happening during those tests. They can simply shunt some percent of API calls to the emulator running on your laptop. That is a big win for them.

Lastly the story of how this was built by hundreds (thousands?) of agents working in parallel is mind blowing.

### [The components of agent memory](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory)

Agents need memory in order to know what they have done and how they have worked with you over time. Memory gets increasingly more sophisticated as the models get more sophisticated. ### [AI and the Future of the Product Role](https://www.svpg.com/ai-and-the-future-of-the-product-role/)

AI is impacting the "future" definition of many roles. And if you aren't as close to this future means about tomorrow. This is all happening incredibly fast. Agents can help us with a lot when it comes to **building** things. They can even help with figuring out what you want to build. However I do think there is still something like "art" here. A sculptor chiseling away at a piece of stone to understand what the stone wants to be. Just because you can create code fast doesn't mean you can figure out what that code wants to be.

> The primary responsibility of an empowered team product manager is to help _discover a solution that will solve the problem and achieve the outcome_.  In order to do this, the product team needs a cross-functional range of skills.

I have a project I believe only now after about six months I've figured out what "it" wants to be. Crazy enough there is no way I could have discovered that by building it before. It would have been incredibly expensive. But now we can discover by experiencing it. This does change fundamentally how we think of product discovery. I actually think it is a whole team thing, not just something handed to the folks with the "product" title. 

> So long as the bottleneck is no longer product delivery, I expect the focus will necessarily be on the skills and techniques to determine the right product to build – the craft of product discovery.  And I expect this will continue to increase the demand for people with this knowledge and skills.

I really want to see folks using the term "building". Building is the thing that we need and it is the thing that is a superpower with AI. In fact, there are plenty of product people that are not builders. You don't have to be a builder to continue a product that has been around for a few years. 

But to take something from nothing to something. That is building. And that is what we need more of. I really don't care if that is called "product" or "developer" or whatever it is.

### [Writing as a thinking tool](https://muratbuffalo.blogspot.com/2026/11/writing-as-thinking-tool.html)

I have shared many times that I think writing is thinking. Also, one of the many reasons I write this email every week is to become a better writer. Writing makes you a better writer. I also believe firmly that when I write it is my words. Even with my ample enthusiasm for what AI enables, I don't want it to write for me because I think that is starving me of my own creative process and critical thinking.

> This is a real problem, and [it seems to be spreading quickly.](https://alifeengineered.substack.com/p/on-the-death-of-amazons-writing-culture) We have been celebrating Amazon's six-pager as a great example of writing-for-thinking. Bezos had said: "There is no way to write a six-page, narratively structured memo and not have clear thinking." Unfortunately, with LLMs, that sentence has stopped being true. You can now produce six pages with no thinking at all, and the six pages still look like evidence that the thinking happened.

I have tried to find ways to have an LLM "assist me" in writing and I haven't found one. Once you see something, you cannot unsee it. The structure that comes from a "preview from AI", at least for me, then forms how I think about the thing no matter what. 

So I still am engaging with my brain first. I'm happy to get editorial feedback from AI on my writing, and there are some places where the writing really isn't personal or important and I can do something different. But when you are learning you aren't really learning if it isn't your brain doing the reps.

**[Wrong Wicks](https://www.thingelstad.com/2026/10/31/wrong-wicks.html)** — I made 120 candles for [our Things 4 Good sale](https://things4good.xyz) with some 0.5 inch wicks. I'm not sure what I was thinking. We typically use 0.75 inch wicks. I did a burn test and it didn't work at all. Ugh! What to do. After some consideration I decided to see if I could use a plier and pull the existing wick straight out. It worked. Now the real challenge — could I channel a new wick back down and seat it in the clip at the bottom. 🤔

It worked! Mostly. Out of 120 candles there were only two that I wasn't able to seat a new wick in. I did a burn test as well since I was concerned the new wick wasn't saturated in hot wax. It worked! Everything burned great.

I'm not going to make that mistake again.
